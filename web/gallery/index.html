brickmap

A voxel rendering engine. This is the build gallery — the latest preview plus frozen snapshots of notable milestones along the way.

liveLatest (main)

The current build, auto-deployed on every push to main.

E3Crystal light

The fake-GI: emissive crystals now cast light, not just glow. A flood-fill bakes their cyan light into the mesh, so it spills onto the terrain in pools, fades with distance, and bleeds around the folds — cheap, baked, no ray tracing.

E3Glow

Light to fly toward: cheap bloom makes the warm ember particles and rare cool emissive crystals glow against the dusk sky. The cheap-light exploration finds its mood — two kinds of glow over an AO-shaded, textured, hazy world.

E3Atmosphere

The world gets air and light: a sky gradient with a hazy horizon the distant terrain dissolves into, and coloured hemispheric ambient (cool sky above, warm bounce below). First slices of the cheap-light exploration.

M4Material & occlusion

It starts to look like brickmap: baked ambient occlusion grounds the terrain (valleys and folds darken), and per-material procedural textures — posterised, tiled per voxel — give surfaces grain without losing the voxel grid. The first deliberate look pass (see the look journal).

M3Endless flight

The world goes infinite: chunks stream in and out around a cinematic cruise camera as it flies, so the terrain never ends — and distance fog melts the far edge into the sky instead of popping in. Debris trails ahead of the flight.

M3Procedural world

Real noise terrain: a 5×5-chunk landscape of rolling hills with snow-capped peaks and sandy lows — plus auto-fly (watch hands-free) and live wobble/dither dials. The first proper *world*.

E2Particles

Voxels that move: ambient bursts of warm emissive cube debris fountain off the terrain and fall. Instanced, cheap, and the start of "atmosphere."

E1Aesthetic pass

The first "personality" pass: the engine's own artifacts become the look — PS1-style vertex wobble + ordered dithering. Low-fi by intent (design §11), not retro pastiche.

M2Greedy terrain

The first real world: a grid of chunks of rolling hills, greedy-meshed (coplanar faces merged into big quads) with chunk seams culled. Far fewer triangles, and you can fly across it.

M1Fly camera

The meshed chunk, now explorable: a free-fly pointer-lock camera (click to look, WASD to move). First time you can move *through* the world rather than watch it spin.

M1First meshed chunk

The first real voxel geometry: a 32³ section meshed by the naïve face-culling mesher and drawn through the world→render contract — a stepped pyramid, lit, with interior faces culled.

M0Spike — single cube

The first proof: clear the screen and draw one lit, tumbling cube via wgpu, on desktop and web from one code path. Validated the cross-platform render path before any voxel code.
